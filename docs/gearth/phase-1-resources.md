## Calibration and limits

The supplied extraction price/output (`30`, `6`) was a first guess. Final settings are **15 minor/unit and 14 units/day per full-strength miner** for both raw goods. Gross revenue at concentration 1 is still 1,470/week; ten energy units at the initial power price cost 250, leaving approximately 1,220 before a 1,000 payroll, assuming full health, full sales and no other costs. Actual health, productivity, utilization, prices and imports change that margin. Normalizing concentration to the specified mean of 1 required 14/day to retain global extraction capacity; these settings need tuning after Ricky's live runs.

Power output is **28/day rather than 22**. Removing the old energy endowment bonus while adding extraction and service power demand left the old capacity too low. Local checks at 22 produced a planned-country fuel/power/hunger feedback loop even with correct input accounting. At 28 the tested year ends with all ten countries' average health above 70. Planned initial power demand is **10 units/resident/week**, including industrial/service power; fuel demand is a quarter of that plus seven units for production rounding. Ore's initial quota is three units/resident/week. These are defaults; planners can change them.

Small production batches previously rounded every input up independently each day. That overcharged a poor mine making only a few units, and made the nominal 1/10 recipe behave much more expensively. Resource input credits now carry the unused capacity of already-consumed inputs into later output. Services use the same principle for ten powered sessions. Credits are part of the saved nation state, not newly created physical goods or money.

The existing no-decision annual calibration retains its health, QoL and unemployment bounds. Planned government/business cash may fall to **15%** of opening capital (formerly 30%), and planned household cash may reach **4x** opening cash (formerly 3x): fixed-price state firms pay the new inputs while households accumulate savings. Other government profiles keep the old cash bounds. The original firm-existence check continues to require every original non-freight sector; mines/wells may close at poor deposits and be replaced by imports, as the resource market is intended to allow. Comments at the assertions explain the changes; no cash is minted to satisfy them.

“Fed” does not mean that every individual eats every meal: the annual resource test checks actual final-month meals above 80% in each country, at least three resident-days of food on domestic shelves at year end, and average resident health above 70. The meal tolerance preserves the original rule-only calibration's allowance for up to 20% unemployment, including monarchies without unemployment benefit. The first stricter 90% meal check found 86.3% in Lebanonia, despite available food and average health above 80; that priced-out hunger is disclosed rather than hidden by changing welfare policy. Zero individual hunger is not established.

The checks use the default seed with no model decisions. Other seeds, government rotations, adversarial quotas, sustained live throughput and year-long model behavior remain to be measured. No Spark, AMD, VM, Netplan or model-server configuration was changed, and no live simulation was started. Resource depletion remains deferred. Legacy worlds without ore/fuel inventories need an integration migration or a fresh world; this branch does not migrate production storage. There are **no stubbed Phase 1 mechanisms**.
